# Fact-check register and official sources

Research reviewed: **14 July 2026**

This file is the editorial safety net. It records what the source supports, what the website may say and what it must not imply. Recheck all volatile facts before launch even if the code has not changed.

## Source hierarchy

For a conflict, prefer in this order:

1. Current Danish law/authority page for residence, CPR, tax or SU.
2. Current official institution page for its own programme, admission, documents and housing.
3. Study in Denmark / Optagelse general guidance.
4. Municipality page for local language/housing/registration details.
5. Independent practical source only when no authority publishes the information, clearly labelled as experience—not a rule.

Do not use a blog, Facebook group, TikTok or student memory to overrule an official current page.

## High-risk claim matrix

| Topic | Safe website wording | Source support | Never say |
|---|---|---|---|
| EU tuition | EU/EEA/Swiss students at Danish public and most recognised institutions generally do not pay tuition; institution decides status | Study in Denmark / SU | “Every EU student studies anywhere in Denmark completely free” |
| SU nature | Ordinary SU is a grant; SU-lån is a separate loan | SU official | “All Danish student money is free and never repaid” |
| SU for EU students | Not automatic; a common route is equal status as an EU worker, individually assessed | SU foreign citizen | “Get a café job and SU is guaranteed” |
| Work amount | General rule: at least 10–12 paid hours each week; normally at least 10 continuous weeks and while receiving SU; personal decision controls | SU “You work in Denmark” | “43 hours a month is enough” |
| Missed hours | May trigger evidence request, loss of worker status, stopped SU and repayment of overpayment; circumstances assessed | SU official | “One missed hour causes a fixed fine” |
| SU rate | 2026 away-from-parents higher-education rate is DKK 7,426/month before tax | SU rates 2026 | Use the number without year/scope |
| Income limit | 2026 lowest monthly component for an SU month in higher education is DKK 20,749 before tax after 8% AM contribution; annual calculation | SU private earnings | “You may earn exactly DKK 20,749 every month with no other considerations” |
| Danish courses | Official Danish education can be course-fee-free for eligible referred residents; local rules and a refundable DKK 2,000 deposit currently apply in Copenhagen/Aarhus | Municipal pages | “All international students get completely free Danish lessons” |
| Bachelor deadline | Applicants with non-Danish upper-secondary qualifications generally apply by 15 March at 12:00 CET | Study in Denmark | “Every Danish university deadline is 15 March” |
| Master deadline | Institution/programme specific; normally university's own portal | University pages | “Master's applications use Optagelse.dk and the same March 15 deadline” |
| Translations | Follow institution. Safe baseline: original + institution-accepted certified English translation where required | AU/institution guidance | “Every document needs an apostille” or “your own translation is always accepted” |
| Housing cash | Deposit and prepaid rent can mean several rents upfront; Copenhagen says up to 3 + 3, plus first rent | City of Copenhagen | “DKK 3,000–10,000 covers every deposit” |
| Moving savings | DKK 25k–75k+ scenarios are our planning inference from rents/upfront costs | Clearly labelled estimate | Present as a legal/official minimum or guarantee |
| EU residence | EU student may enter/study/work; if staying over three months, apply for EU residence document; individual conditions apply | SIRI | “EU citizens do not have to register anything” |
| Programme catalogue | Discovery list only; Study in Denmark says overview is being revised; verify at institution | Study in Denmark | “This is a complete guaranteed current list” |

## Admissions sources

### General Bachelor's process

[Study in Denmark — How to apply](https://studyindenmark.dk/study-options/how-to-apply)

Supports:

- undergraduate applications through Optagelse.dk;
- up to eight programmes;
- one offer at the highest possible priority for which admission is possible;
- foreign/non-Danish qualification deadline 15 March at 12:00 CET;
- priority changes and some uploads until 5 July at 12:00, subject to institution requirements;
- decisions on 28 July;
- without MitID, a signature page for each programme must be printed, hand-signed and sent separately to the institution using its required channel;
- uploaded diplomas/documents and signature-page handling are distinct.

Editorial note: Optagelse labels and screens can change. Describe the stable sequence and use screenshots only after a same-season visual recheck.

### Quota 1 / Quota 2

[Study in Denmark — Regulations of admission](https://studyindenmark.dk/study-options/how-to-apply/regulations-of-admission-quota-1-and-quota-2)

Use to verify the current general distinction and how foreign applicants submitted by 15 March are considered. Institution-specific Quota 2 criteria must come from the programme.

### Master's example and proof that deadlines differ

[University of Copenhagen — Master's application and admission](https://www.ku.dk/studies/masters/application-and-admission)

At the research date, UCPH gives EU/EEA/Swiss applicants a 1 March at 23:59 deadline for the relevant autumn intake and an answer by 15 May. Use only as a UCPH example; do not generalise.

### Czech qualification example

[University of Southern Denmark — Czech Republic](https://www.sdu.dk/en/uddannelse/bachelor/bachelor-admission/admission-requirements/educational-background/qualifying-exam-eu/czech-republic)

Supports that a Czech `Vysvědčení o maturitní zkoušce` can be a qualifying examination and illustrates commonly requested materials such as the final certificate, results for the last three years and a statement of hours. Vocational routes and subject equivalence can carry additional conditions.

Editorial note: phrase the list as “often requested / check your programme,” not a Denmark-wide universal checklist.

### Slovak qualification example

SDU's country-specific entrance-qualification document at the research date lists `Vysvedčenie o maturitnej skúške`, results from the last three years and programme-dependent material such as course descriptions/teaching hours. Re-locate the current country page or PDF before launch because document URLs can change.

### Translation standard example

[Aarhus University — Gather your documentation](https://masters.au.dk/how-to-apply/gather-documentation)

Supports:

- documents not in Danish, Norwegian, Swedish or English require translation for that AU process;
- diploma translations should come from the issuing university/school or be signed/stamped by an authorised translator;
- AU may accept self-translated course descriptions with originals, but this does not prove that other universities do.

Safe site rule: original + certified/official English translation when the institution requires translation; verify institution-specific acceptance. Do not prescribe apostille/legalisation unless requested.

## Programme and education sources

### Catalogue

[Study in Denmark — Programmes](https://studyindenmark.dk/portal)

The live catalogue currently states that it contains English-taught programmes and warns that Study in Denmark and the overview are being revised. The supplied data was generated from its public catalogue on 14 July 2026:

- source catalogue: 458 entries of all represented types;
- included here: 352 entries carrying the catalogue's Bachelor (`29`) or Master (`30`) degree codes;
- 53 Bachelor's and 299 Master's discovery records.

This count is a snapshot, not a marketing promise. Some records have stale historical intake data; the update script drops past dates, and the UI must still verify current dates with the institution.

### Education style

- [Study in Denmark — Study in English](https://studyindenmark.dk/study-in-denmark/education-in-denmark/study-in-english)
- [Study in Denmark — Bachelor's programmes](https://studyindenmark.dk/study-options/what-can-i-study/bachelors-degree-programmes)
- [Study in Denmark — Education overview](https://studyindenmark.dk/study-in-denmark)

These support English-taught study, internationally oriented programmes and the broad Danish emphasis on critical thinking, collaboration, creativity, project work and practical application. Avoid saying every course uses exactly the same pedagogy.

### Tuition

- [Study in Denmark — Tuition fees and scholarships](https://studyindenmark.dk/study-options/tuition-fees-and-scholarships)
- [SU — State Educational Grant and Loan Scheme](https://www.su.dk/english/state-educational-grant-and-loan-scheme-su)

The SU overview explicitly says tuition at Danish public and most private institutions is free for Danish and EU/EEA students. The institution decides actual tuition status.

## SU sources

### EU worker status

[SU — You work in Denmark](https://www.su.dk/foreign-citizen/gb-foreign-citizen/eu-rules/you-work-in-denmark)

Key source. Supports:

- genuine paid employment;
- general 10–12 hours per week;
- exactly 40 hours/month generally insufficient because most months exceed four weeks;
- normally a continuous period of at least 10 weeks and continuing while SU is received;
- individual assessment of wage and employment terms;
- personal approval letter states required weekly hours;
- first-ten-weeks holiday risk;
- ongoing monitoring via employer/tax data;
- keeping contracts, payslips, signed timesheets/schedules and holiday documentation;
- immediate reporting of changes.

Copy review rule: search the whole site for `43`. The only acceptable uses explain why it is not the official safe rule.

### Application process

[SU — Foreign citizen: how to apply](https://www.su.dk/foreign-citizen/gb-foreign-citizen/foreign-citizen-how-to-apply)

Supports:

- CPR and school registration;
- minSU application plus foreign-citizen equal-status form;
- equal-status form within three weeks;
- use a computer rather than a mobile device;
- earliest SU month is the application month, not earlier;
- application by the last day of the desired month and annual deadline 5 December;
- applying up to three months before entitlement in relevant situations;
- new application when moving from Bachelor's to Master's.

### 2026 SU amount

[SU — 2026 higher-education rate for students living away from parents](https://www.su.dk/satser/videregaaende-uddannelser-satser-for-su-til-udeboende)

Supports DKK 7,426/month before tax for the stated category. Replace or expire the displayed rate automatically for 2027 unless the new official rate is entered.

### Income limit

[SU — Rules regarding employment and private earnings](https://www.su.dk/english/state-educational-grant-and-loan-scheme-su/rules-regarding-employment-and-private-earnings)

Supports:

- annual `fribeløb` calculation;
- 2026 lowest monthly component of DKK 20,749 for higher-education months receiving SU;
- number is before tax but after 8% labour-market contribution;
- different month categories and special rules;
- repayment if annual limit is exceeded.

[SU — Scheme overview](https://www.su.dk/english/state-educational-grant-and-loan-scheme-su)

Use the live official wording for any interest/repayment surcharge. Do not hardcode a penalty percentage unless rechecked for the exact situation.

### 2027 reform

[SU — Reform in English](https://www.su.dk/su-reform/su-reform-in-english)

Supports, for students who start a new higher education on/after 1 July 2025 from January 2027:

- overall support framework reducing from 70 to 58 portions;
- support aligned to standard programme duration rather than an automatic extra 12;
- completion-loan changes;
- additional portions for specified groups such as certain disabled students and single parents.

Recheck before January 2027 and when translating exceptions.

## Danish language-course sources

- [City of Copenhagen — Danish Language Education Programme](https://international.kk.dk/live/learn-danish/danish-language-courses/danish-language-education-programme)
- [Aarhus Municipality — Learn Danish for free](https://international.aarhus.dk/live/learn-danish-for-free-the-official-danish-education)

## Residence, CPR and arrival sources

### EU student residence

[SIRI — EU student](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB/You-want-to-apply/Residence-as-a-Nordic-citizen-or-EU-or-EEA-citizen/EU-student)

Supports:

- fully admitted active study at an accredited programme;
- declaration of sufficient means;
- no fee for the EU document route;
- EU citizen can enter, study and work immediately without a work permit;
- over-three-month stay requires an EU residence document;
- residence document is needed for CPR registration.

[SIRI — OD1/A EU residence form](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/-/media/Files/SIRI/Application-forms/EU/OD1A_en.pdf)

Supports personal appearance and original identity-document/checklist handling. Forms and process can change; link to the live SIRI page first.

### CPR, health card, MitID and tax sequence

- [Life in Denmark — When you arrive](https://lifeindenmark.borger.dk/theme/when-you-arrive)
- [International Citizen Service East / Copenhagen](https://lifeindenmark.borger.dk/settle-in-denmark/ics-international-citizen-service/ics-east-in-copenhagen)
- [Life in Denmark — NemKonto](https://lifeindenmark.borger.dk/apps-and-digital-services/nemkonto-your-public-bank-account)

These support the practical sequence of EU residence → CPR/address registration → MitID/Digital Post → health card/bank/NemKonto/tax. Local appointment/document requirements differ.

## Housing and budget sources

### General budget

[Study in Denmark — Bank & Budget](https://studyindenmark.dk/live-in-denmark/bank-budget)

Published rough monthly ranges at research date:

- rent DKK 3,000–6,500;
- food DKK 2,000–3,500;
- insurance around DKK 300;
- books/supplies DKK 400–650;
- transport around DKK 300;
- personal expenses around DKK 2,000.

Use as an orientation, not a promise. Copenhagen can be substantially higher.

### Deposit and prepaid rent

- [Life in Denmark — Renting a home](https://lifeindenmark.borger.dk/housing-and-moving/rental-property/renting-a-home)
- [City of Copenhagen — Average renting costs](https://international.kk.dk/live/housing/finding-a-place-to-live/average-renting-costs)

Supports typical room/flat deposit differences and the potential for up to three months' deposit + three months' prepaid rent. The Copenhagen page plus first rent yields the illustrative maximum of seven months' rent upfront. This is why a DKK 10,000 universal moving budget is unsafe.

### Copenhagen

[University of Copenhagen — Housing](https://www.ku.dk/studies/student-life/housing)

Supports `s.dk`/`mit.s.dk`, KKIK, current broad rent indication and early search. At research date UCPH describes Copenhagen housing around DKK 4,000–10,000+ and common multiple-month upfront payments.

- [s.dk / mit.s.dk](https://mit.s.dk)
- [KKIK](https://www.kollegierneskontor.dk)

### Aarhus

[Aarhus University — Student housing in Aarhus](https://international.au.dk/life/locations/housing/auhousing/student-housing-aarhus)

Supports:

- [StudentHousingAarhus.com](https://studenthousingaarhus.com) as central portal;
- free registration from age 17 without admission documents at registration;
- seniority and activation roughly three months before desired move-in;
- broad selections/outer areas improve chances;
- tenancy requires meeting active-student/age conditions;
- common upfront payment around 4–6 months' rent in the described setting.

Housing guarantees have conditions and deadlines. Never state one without checking the applicant category and intake.

### Odense

[SDU — Housing in Odense](https://www.sdu.dk/en/uddannelse/studenthousing/odense)

At research date supports:

- housing guarantee for eligible international students applying on time;
- 1 May 23:59 for September and 1 November for February;
- applying before final admission is accepted;
- [SDU Housing portal](https://sduhousing.sdu.dk).

Check the year/intake at every publication cycle.

### Aalborg

[Aalborg University — Accommodation for students in Aalborg](https://www.en.aau.dk/living-in-denmark/accommodation/international-students-in-aalborg)

Supports using the AAU Accommodation Office, available housing types and the statement that rent is paid three months at a time in that offering. The visible price range was marked edited March 2024 at research time, so do not surface it as a 2026/current price without new confirmation.

## Planning-estimate derivation

The moving buffers in the copy are inferences, not direct official numbers:

- Low scenario: confirmed lower-rent student housing + modest upfront payment + approximately two months' living costs.
- General safe scenario: broader rent, several months upfront and 2–3 months before reliable job/SU.
- Copenhagen/private scenario: DKK 4,000–10,000+ rent, potentially 6 months deposit/prepaid + first month, plus living/travel/setup.

The calculator is better than a single claim. Always allow the student to replace assumptions with contract values.

## Launch fact-check checklist

### Every January

- [ ] New SU monthly rate entered with year/source.
- [ ] New `fribeløb` rates entered and annual-calculation copy reviewed.
- [ ] SU reform/worker rule checked for wording changes.
- [ ] Bachelor cycle dates confirmed.
- [ ] Danish course deposit/eligibility checked in named cities.
- [ ] City housing deadlines checked.
- [ ] Old dates automatically expired from UI.

### Every admissions cycle, before 1 February

- [ ] Optagelse screenshots and signature-page workflow tested.
- [ ] General deadlines checked.
- [ ] Czech and Slovak country-document pages rechecked.
- [ ] Each institution's Master portal/deadline links reviewed.
- [ ] Programme catalogue regenerated and diff reviewed.
- [ ] Removed programmes handled in saved lists.

### Monthly November–March; quarterly otherwise

- [ ] Programme updater run.
- [ ] Broken official programme URLs sampled/reported.
- [ ] Housing portal links checked.
- [ ] SIRI/CPR link paths checked.
- [ ] Correction inbox reviewed.

### Before any paid promotion

- [ ] No “free money” wording.
- [ ] No guaranteed admission/SU/housing.
- [ ] No unqualified “free Danish courses.”
- [ ] Planning estimate clearly labelled.
- [ ] Independent status visible.
- [ ] Founder/team claims approved by each person.
